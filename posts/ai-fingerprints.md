# The Tool Leaves Fingerprints

A few months ago I built an intelligence dashboard for the city of The Hague. Monitors, live event feeds, city statistics. I asked an AI to come up with a theme and it landed on something Watch Dogs-inspired: dark backgrounds, clean geometric elements, subtle animations that made the whole thing feel like actual surveillance infrastructure. It looked great. I was proud of it.

I showed it to a colleague the next day. He looked at it for a few seconds and said it looked very typical for an AI generated website. Then he walked me through it: the layout, the card elements, the animation style. He'd seen the fingerprint before on other things built with the same tools. Not my dashboard, just the same stamp.

That feeling of going from proud to deflated in thirty seconds is hard to describe.

The thing about that feeling is that once you've had it, you can't un-have it. A few days ago a friend showed me his new website, excited the way you are when something comes out better than you expected. I recognised the fingerprint immediately. Same visual language, slightly different colors. I didn't say anything. But I felt like a party pooper for the rest of the evening just for noticing.

The uncomfortable part isn't that AI generates slop. It's that it generates slop that looks impressive until you've seen enough of it. We're all reaching into the same bag and pulling out slightly different coloured versions of the same thing, and for a while each one feels like a creative achievement.

Most people don't see it yet. They will though. And then we'll all be party poopers together.
